About aeration, sod & seeding

A strong lawn starts below the surface. Whether you're starting from bare soil or bringing back a thin, patchy yard, the results depend on how well the ground is prepared and how the new grass is cared for in its first weeks.

Seed is the budget-friendly route but needs time to fill in. Sod costs more and looks finished the day it goes down. We'll walk you through both options and handle the work either way.

Why aerate?

Two things hold most lawns back: compacted soil and a thick layer of thatch. Core aeration pulls small plugs of soil out of the lawn, opening space for air, water, and roots and breaking up both problems.

The plugs left on the surface dissolve back into the lawn within a week or two, which helps keep thatch in check. Aeration pays off most as a yearly habit rather than a one-time fix.

  • Better drainage after heavy rain
  • Air, water, and nutrients reach the roots
  • Looser, less compacted soil
  • New root and shoot growth
  • Less thatch build-up
  • Roots reach water more easily in dry spells
  • An ideal seedbed for overseeding
  • Fertilizer that actually reaches the roots

Fall is the top choice for aeration, with spring as a second option. Once a year is a good rule of thumb for most lawns.

Why overseed?

Overseeding puts new grass where old grass has thinned out. Combined with aeration and a light top dressing, it helps a lawn come back thicker and greener.

Right after aeration is the best moment to seed, since seed settles into the open holes and loosened soil. The pairing is an affordable way to revive a lawn worn down by a wet spring or a dry summer, and doing both in early fall gives new grass time to root before winter.

Caring for a newly seeded lawn

  1. Leave the plugs

    Don't rake them up. They break apart on their own and fall back into the holes, often helped along by your mower within two to three weeks.

  2. Keep the surface damp

    Water right after seeding, then keep the top of the soil damp, not soggy, for about three weeks. After that, water more deeply but less often, ideally early in the morning.

  3. Stay off it

    Keep people and pets off seeded areas for the first few weeks so young seedlings aren't crushed.

  4. First mowing

    Wait until new grass is about 4 inches tall. Use a sharp blade, cut no more than a third of the height at a time, and leave the clippings.

  5. Leaves on new grass

    Raking can tear out young seedlings. Blow leaves off lightly or mulch them with the mower instead.

All FAQs by service
How soon can I use my new lawn?

That depends on whether you choose seed or sod, and on the weather. We'll give you a realistic timeline before we start.

When is the best time to seed?

Early fall is usually the best window. Spring can work too. We'll recommend timing for your yard.

When is the best time to aerate?

Fall is the top choice, with spring as a second option. Once a year is a good rule of thumb, and fall lets you pair aeration with overseeding.

Should I pick up the soil plugs after aeration?

No. Leave them on the lawn. They break down within a week or two and work back into the soil.

Can you repair just part of my lawn?

Yes. Patching bare or thin areas is a common job.

Does the ground need work before seeding or sod?

Often, yes. New lawns usually need soil prep and some grading first. If water pools in your yard, tell us about it so we can plan around it.

Should I choose seed or sod?

Seed costs less but takes weeks to fill in. Sod costs more and gives you a finished-looking lawn right away. We'll walk you through the tradeoffs for your yard and budget.

How much does aeration, seeding, or sod cost?

It depends on the size of the area, its condition, and whether you choose seed or sod. Call or text (610) 420-3338 for a quote.

How should I water new seed?

Water right after seeding, then keep the soil surface damp, not soggy, for about three weeks. After that, water more deeply but less often, ideally early in the morning.

How should I water new sod?

New sod needs frequent watering at first so its roots knit into the soil below. We'll give you a watering plan when it's installed.

When can I mow new grass?

Wait until it's about 4 inches tall. Use a sharp blade and cut no more than a third of the height at a time.

Can you aerate and overseed in the same visit?

Yes. Seed takes best right after aeration, so the two are often done together.

Do you offer this for businesses and HOAs?

Ask us. We serve residential, commercial, and multi-living properties.

How do I handle mowing after new seed or sod?

Ask us how to coordinate mowing with your new lawn so young grass isn't damaged.
