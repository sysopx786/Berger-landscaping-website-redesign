Berger Landscaping is a family-owned landscaping business proudly serving Pottstown and the surrounding communities.

![Drew Berger, owner of Berger Landscaping, with a backpack leaf blower](https://sysopx786.github.io/Berger-landscaping-website-redesign/docs/img/berger-drew-01-1024.jpg)

## Meet Drew Berger

Welcome to Berger Landscaping LLC! The company was founded by Drew Berger, a devoted Penn State alumnus. It's built on a foundation of experience in business management and a passion for creating beautiful outdoor spaces. Drew is dedicated to delivering meticulous and personalized landscaping solutions for every project.

Outside of work, he enjoys time spent with his wife and three children, serves as a deacon at a local church, and is an avid hunter and fisherman. We deeply appreciate your ongoing support, which helps our growing family and allows us to make a positive impact in our community.

Thank you for being an essential part of the Berger Landscaping LLC family!

## Mowing

**How often will you mow my lawn?**

Most lawns do best with a regular weekly visit during the growing season. We can adjust the schedule for slower growth in spring and fall or for your property's needs.

**What is included in each mowing visit?**

Mowing, edging along walkways and beds, trimming around obstacles, and blowing clippings off driveways and hard surfaces.

**Do you mow residential and commercial properties?**

Yes. We serve homes, businesses, and multi-living communities.

**Can you mow a large lawn or a steep hill?**

Yes. We take on challenging properties, including large lawns and steep hills.

**Do I need to be home when you mow?**

No. As long as we can reach the lawn, you don't need to be there. Just let us know about locked gates or pets.

**What if it rains on my mowing day?**

We'll reschedule for the next suitable day. Cutting very wet grass can tear it and leave ruts.

**How short do you cut the grass?**

We cut at a height suited to your grass type and the season. Mowing too short stresses a lawn and invites weeds and heat damage.

**Do you bag the clippings?**

We can discuss this when you set up service. By default, clippings are cleared from driveways, walkways, and hard surfaces.

**Can you get rid of weeds in my lawn while you mow?**

Regular, properly timed mowing helps crowd out weeds. For heavy weed problems, ask us about the best approach for your lawn.

**Do I have to sign a contract for mowing?**

Ask us about our current terms. Call or text (610) 420-3338 and we'll explain how service works.

**How do I get a mowing quote?**

Call or text (610) 420-3338, or use the quote form on our Contact page with your address and a bit about your lawn.

**Can you start mid-season?**

Usually yes. Contact us with your address and we'll let you know when we can add you to the schedule.

**Will you mow around my flower beds, trees, and play equipment?**

Yes. We trim and edge around beds, trees, fences, and other obstacles as part of the visit.

**Do you offer one-time mowing, such as before an event or a home sale?**

Please ask. We can often help with a one-time cut when our schedule allows.

**Are you licensed and insured?**

Yes. Berger Landscaping LLC is fully licensed and insured.

Spring and fall are the most popular times, but we can help whenever your yard needs a reset.

Clearing leaves and debris before winter helps prevent mold, fungus, and pests, and protects your lawn through the cold months.

**Why do a spring clean-up?**

**Do you do clean-ups for rental or vacant properties?**

Yes. Contact us with the address and what's needed.

**Do I need to be home?**

No, as long as we can access the yard.

**How much does a clean-up cost?**

It depends on yard size and condition. Call or text (610) 420-3338 for a quote.

**Will a clean-up help my lawn?**

Yes. Removing weeds and debris lets the lawn breathe and helps it stay healthy into the next season.

**Can you combine a clean-up with leaf removal or mulching?**

Yes. We can bundle services into one visit.

**Why do you put a mulch ring around new trees?**

## Aeration, Sod & Seeding

**How soon can I use my new lawn?**

That depends on whether you choose seed or sod, and on the weather. We'll give you a realistic timeline before we start.

**When is the best time to seed?**

Early fall is usually the best window. Spring can work too. We'll recommend timing for your yard.

**When is the best time to aerate?**

Fall is the top choice, with spring as a second option. Once a year is a good rule of thumb, and fall lets you pair aeration with overseeding.

**Should I pick up the soil plugs after aeration?**

No. Leave them on the lawn. They break down within a week or two and work back into the soil.

**Can you repair just part of my lawn?**

Yes. Patching bare or thin areas is a common job.

**Does the ground need work before seeding or sod?**

Often, yes. New lawns usually need soil prep and some grading first. If water pools in your yard, tell us about it so we can plan around it.

**Should I choose seed or sod?**

Seed costs less but takes weeks to fill in. Sod costs more and gives you a finished-looking lawn right away. We'll walk you through the tradeoffs for your yard and budget.

**How much does aeration, seeding, or sod cost?**

It depends on the size of the area, its condition, and whether you choose seed or sod. Call or text (610) 420-3338 for a quote.

**How should I water new seed?**

Water right after seeding, then keep the soil surface damp, not soggy, for about three weeks. After that, water more deeply but less often, ideally early in the morning.

**How should I water new sod?**

New sod needs frequent watering at first so its roots knit into the soil below. We'll give you a watering plan when it's installed.

**When can I mow new grass?**

Wait until it's about 4 inches tall. Use a sharp blade and cut no more than a third of the height at a time.

**Can you aerate and overseed in the same visit?**

Yes. Seed takes best right after aeration, so the two are often done together.

**Do you offer this for businesses and HOAs?**

Ask us. We serve residential, commercial, and multi-living properties.

**How do I handle mowing after new seed or sod?**

Ask us how to coordinate mowing with your new lawn so young grass isn't damaged.

# Aeration, Sod & Seeding

Thicker, healthier turf, whether you're patching bare spots or starting a lawn from bare soil.

Call or text (610) 420-3338Request a quote

## About aeration, sod & seeding

A strong lawn starts below the surface. Whether you're starting from bare soil or bringing back a thin, patchy yard, the results depend on how well the ground is prepared and how the new grass is cared for in its first weeks.

Seed is the budget-friendly route but needs time to fill in. Sod costs more and looks finished the day it goes down. We'll walk you through both options and handle the work either way.

## Why aerate?

Two things hold most lawns back: compacted soil and a thick layer of thatch. Core aeration pulls small plugs of soil out of the lawn, opening space for air, water, and roots and breaking up both problems.

The plugs left on the surface dissolve back into the lawn within a week or two, which helps keep thatch in check. Aeration pays off most as a yearly habit rather than a one-time fix.

- Better drainage after heavy rain

- Air, water, and nutrients reach the roots

- Looser, less compacted soil

- New root and shoot growth

- Less thatch build-up

- Roots reach water more easily in dry spells

- An ideal seedbed for overseeding

- Fertilizer that actually reaches the roots

Fall is the top choice for aeration, with spring as a second option. Once a year is a good rule of thumb for most lawns.

## Why overseed?

Overseeding puts new grass where old grass has thinned out. Combined with aeration and a light top dressing, it helps a lawn come back thicker and greener.

Right after aeration is the best moment to seed, since seed settles into the open holes and loosened soil. The pairing is an affordable way to revive a lawn worn down by a wet spring or a dry summer, and doing both in early fall gives new grass time to root before winter.

## Caring for a newly seeded lawn

-

### Leave the plugs

Don't rake them up. They break apart on their own and fall back into the holes, often helped along by your mower within two to three weeks.

-

### Keep the surface damp

Water right after seeding, then keep the top of the soil damp, not soggy, for about three weeks. After that, water more deeply but less often, ideally early in the morning.

-

### Stay off it

Keep people and pets off seeded areas for the first few weeks so young seedlings aren't crushed.

-

### First mowing

Wait until new grass is about 4 inches tall. Use a sharp blade, cut no more than a third of the height at a time, and leave the clippings.

-

### Leaves on new grass

Raking can tear out young seedlings. Blow leaves off lightly or mulch them with the mower instead.

**When is the best time to plant a tree?**

In Pennsylvania, spring and fall are generally the best planting windows, when temperatures are mild. We'll recommend timing for your trees.

**Why do you put a mulch ring around new trees?**

A mulch ring holds moisture, keeps weeds down, moderates soil temperature, and keeps mowers and string trimmers away from the trunk.

**Can mulch be piled up against the trunk?**

No. Mulch piled against the bark (sometimes called a "mulch volcano") holds moisture on the trunk and can cause rot. Mulch should be spread wide and kept back from the bark.

**Can you plant evergreens for privacy?**

Yes. We plant rows of evergreens along fence lines and property edges to create a screen.

**How far apart should privacy evergreens be planted?**

It depends on the variety and how quickly you want a full screen. We'll recommend spacing based on the trees you choose.

**How often should I water a new tree?**

New trees need regular, deep watering through their first growing season, and more during dry spells. Ask us for guidance on your specific trees.

**Do you guarantee the trees you plant?**

Ask us about our current terms.

**Can you remove a dead or unwanted tree?**

We remove plants and shrubbery as part of our trimming service. For large trees, tell us about the situation and we'll let you know what we can handle or whether a specialist is needed.

**Can you trim my existing trees?**

**Do you need to check for buried utilities before digging?**
